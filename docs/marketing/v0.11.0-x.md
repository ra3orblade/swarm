Swarm v0.11.0 is out.

I built a provenance view this week - issue → task → session → branch → PR, mostly as an audit toy. Then I pointed it at my own machine.

22 branches had landed with no ticket behind them. One was a merged PR carrying $255 and 16 agent sessions. I could not have told you it existed.

That is the release, really. The dashboard used to tell you what your agents did. Now it tells you what it cost, in money, in waiting, in context, and in work nobody can trace back.

## Thread (optional follow-ups)

1. The trick was walking the chain from both ends. Forward from tasks tells you "did this ticket produce anything". Backward from branches tells you what shipped with no ticket at all — and a task-keyed walk can never surface that, because there is no task to start from.

2. My first version ranked chains by how incomplete they were. That buried the 22 real findings under 93 roadmap rows nobody had ever claimed. Ranking by audit severity, then by money, put them back on top — the data told me the ordering was wrong.

3. Same week, the context view found a session that spent 11% of its window re-reading the same diff. Ten times. Reading a file once is work; the other nine copies are what forgetting costs.

4. A/B dispatch also landed: one task, N models, side by side on cost, wall time, gates and diff size. It wanted me to relax the one-holder claim so two runs could share a task. I didn't — each arm claims `task#model` and gets its own worktree. An experiment is not a reason to weaken a fail-closed rule.

5. An arm only wins if it finished *and* passed every gate it ran. Among those, cheapest wins. A cheap arm that failed a gate never wins — the cheap wrong answer is not the answer.
